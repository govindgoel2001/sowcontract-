# Pricing Models

A short guide for choosing how to price an AI agency engagement. The
`proposal` and `sow` skills use this when the user is unsure.

---

## Fixed price

One agreed price for a defined scope.

- **Use when:** scope is clear and stable, project is well understood.
- **Pros:** client knows the cost; rewards you for working efficiently.
- **Risk:** scope creep eats your margin. Only works with a tight SOW and a
  real Change Request process.
- **Structure:** advance + milestone payments. Common split: 40% advance,
  30% midpoint, 30% on final acceptance.

## Milestone-based

Fixed price, released in tranches as defined milestones are accepted.

- **Use when:** larger projects, or a client who wants to see progress before
  paying more.
- **Pros:** cash flow through the project; lowers client risk so it closes
  bigger deals.
- **Structure:** tie each payment to acceptance of a specific deliverable,
  not to a calendar date.

## Monthly retainer

A fixed monthly fee for ongoing work or capacity.

- **Use when:** maintenance, continuous improvement, "AI team on call", or
  managed automation.
- **Pros:** predictable recurring revenue; the most valuable model long term.
- **Structure:** define an included scope or hours per month; extra work is
  billed separately or rolls over. Set a minimum commitment (e.g. 3 months).

## Hourly / time-and-materials

Bill for time spent at an agreed rate.

- **Use when:** scope genuinely cannot be defined up front, or for discovery
  and exploratory work.
- **Pros:** you are never underpaid for the work done.
- **Risk:** client dislikes open-ended cost. Cap it with a "not to exceed"
  ceiling and weekly reporting.

## Value / outcome-based

Price tied to the result (e.g. a share of cost saved or revenue added).

- **Use when:** you can measure the outcome and trust the client's numbers.
- **Pros:** highest upside.
- **Risk:** hard to measure, hard to enforce, slow to collect. Treat as
  advanced; pair with a base fee so you are never working for free.

---

## Recommendation for a new AI agency

- **First projects:** fixed price with milestone payments. Predictable for
  both sides, and it forces you to write a tight SOW.
- **Pilots / proofs of concept:** small fixed-price "discovery" engagement
  first, then quote the full build. Reduces client risk and your scoping risk.
- **After delivery:** convert clients to a monthly retainer for maintenance
  and improvements. This is where stable agency revenue comes from.
- **Always:** take an advance, exclude AI API/token costs from your fee
  (bill at cost or use client keys), and charge extra work through a signed
  Change Request.

## Common mistakes

- Pricing the hours, not the value. A 2-day automation that saves the client
  ₹5 lakh a year is not a 2-day price.
- No advance. Always take one.
- Absorbing token costs. They are variable; pass them through.
- Quoting before scope is written. Quote off the SOW, not off a vague brief.
- Forgetting taxes. State fees as exclusive of GST so you are covered when
  you register.
