# What To Include — Checklist

What every document in this toolkit must contain, and why. This is the
answer to "tell me what all to include". The skills use it; you can also read
it directly before a client call.

---

## Proposal — what to include

A proposal sells. It is read by a busy person deciding whether to trust you.
Keep it 2-4 pages.

| Section | Why it matters |
|---------|----------------|
| Cover / header | Client name, your name, date, a validity date, a reference number. Looks professional and dated. |
| The problem | Stated in the client's words. Proves you listened. Skipping this makes you sound like a vendor, not a partner. |
| Proposed solution | The outcome they get. Lead with the result, not the tech stack. |
| Scope summary | The boundary of the work at a glance. Full detail goes in the SOW. |
| Deliverables | A short list of concrete things they receive. |
| Timeline / phases | Realistic duration and phases. Note the start depends on signing + advance. |
| Investment | Price in INR, the pricing model, the payment schedule. State "exclusive of taxes". |
| Why us | Relevant proof. One real result with a number beats a paragraph of adjectives. |
| Assumptions | What your estimate depends on. Protects the price. |
| Not included | Explicit exclusions. Prevents "but I thought that was part of it". |
| Validity | A date the price expires. Creates a reason to decide. |
| Call to action | One clear next step and who to contact. |

Leave out: full legal terms (those go in the contract), and a wall of
technical detail (that goes in the SOW).

## Scope of Work — what to include

The SOW defines and controls the work. It is the document you both point to
in a disagreement. Be precise; ambiguity here costs you money.

| Section | Why it matters |
|---------|----------------|
| Parties & references | Legal names matching the contract; a SOW number; effective date. |
| Link to the Service Agreement | The SOW sits under the MSA as Annexure A; state which document wins on a conflict. |
| Project objective | The single outcome that defines success. |
| Deliverables | Each one described concretely, with a format and handover method. |
| Acceptance criteria | Per deliverable, an objective testable condition for sign-off. A deliverable without this is a future argument. |
| Out of scope | An explicit exclusion list. Your strongest protection against scope creep. |
| Milestones & timeline | Dates or week-offsets from a defined start date. |
| Acceptance process | Review window, what counts as accepted, what happens on silence. |
| Client responsibilities | Access, data, content, approvals, a single named point of contact. |
| Dependencies & assumptions | What the timeline relies on; client-caused delay shifts the dates. |
| AI / tools & usage costs | Models used, who pays API/token costs, accuracy position, human-review duty. |
| Revisions policy | How many revision rounds are included per deliverable. |
| Change request process | Written + signed before extra work starts; the rate for extra work. |
| Payment schedule | Milestones mapped to acceptance, amounts in INR. |
| Sign-off block | Names, designations, signatures, dates. |

## Service Agreement (MSA) — what to include

The umbrella legal contract. The SOW attaches to it. This is binding — have a
lawyer review it.

| Clause | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Parties & recitals | Legal names, entity types, addresses, signatories, PAN; why the parties are contracting. |
| Definitions | Capitalised defined terms used consistently throughout. |
| Scope via SOW | Work is only binding if in a signed SOW; no SOW, no obligation. |
| Term | When it starts, how long it runs. |
| Fees & payment | Amounts, advance, invoice due window, late interest, suspension right. |
| Taxes | GST (you are unregistered — fees exclusive of tax, GST added on registration) and TDS handling with your PAN. |
| Intellectual property | Deliverables assigned on full payment; your Pre-Existing IP stays yours and is licensed. |
| Confidentiality | Mutual, with standard carve-outs, surviving termination. |
| Data protection | DPDP Act 2023 roles: client is Data Fiduciary, you are Data Processor. |
| AI-specific terms | Third-party providers, no accuracy warranty, client review duty, training-data opt-out, prompt/IP split, acceptable use. See `ai-agency-clauses.md`. |
| Warranties & disclaimer | Reasonable skill and care; everything else "as is". |
| Limitation of liability | Capped at fees paid; no indirect/consequential loss. The clause that protects your survival. |
| Indemnity | Each side covers claims caused by its own breach or misuse. |
| Termination | For convenience (notice period) and for cause (cure period); what survives. |
| Force majeure | Covers AI/internet outages and events beyond control. |
| Independent contractor | You are not an employee; no partnership or agency. |
| Non-solicitation | Neither side poaches the other's people. |
| Governing law & disputes | Indian law, arbitration seat, court jurisdiction. |
| Notices | How formal notice is given and when it takes effect. |
| Boilerplate | Entire agreement, amendment, severability, waiver, assignment, counterparts/e-signature. |
| Signature block + Annexure A | Signatures; the SOW attached. |

## NDA — what to include

Signed before you share proposals or receive client data. Default to mutual.

| Clause | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Parties & purpose | Who, and what the information is being shared for. |
| Definition of Confidential Information | Broad, with the standard four carve-outs (public, already known, independently developed, lawfully received). |
| Receiving party obligations | Use only for the purpose; limited disclosure; reasonable care; no training AI models on it. |
| Compelled disclosure | What happens if a court orders disclosure. |
| No licence / no obligation | Sharing info grants no IP rights and forces no deal. |
| Term & return | How long obligations last; return or destroy on request. |
| Remedies | Injunctive relief, because money may not undo a leak. |
| Governing law & disputes | Indian law, arbitration, jurisdiction. |
| Signature block | Names, designations, signatures, dates. |

---

## The order to send documents

1. **NDA** — before sharing anything sensitive or receiving client data.
2. **Proposal** — to win the deal.
3. **Service Agreement + SOW (Annexure A)** — to formalise it. The client
   signs both together; the advance invoice follows.
4. **Change Requests** — signed addenda to the SOW for any new work.

## Before you sign anything

- Legal names are identical across the proposal, SOW, NDA, and MSA.
- Every placeholder `[IN BRACKETS]` is filled.
- Numbers add up: deliverables in the proposal match the SOW; payment
  milestones in the SOW match the MSA.
- A qualified Indian advocate has reviewed the contract and NDA for any
  high-value or high-risk deal. These templates are a strong starting point,
  not a substitute for legal advice.
